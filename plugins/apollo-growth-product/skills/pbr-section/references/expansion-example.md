# **Growth Expansion**

## *How are we driving account expansion through seat growth, credit purchases and plan upgrades?*

**DRI:** Sourabh Ahuja

### Q1 Strategy

Seat expansion is the primary lever this quarter, with credit and tier-up opportunities picked up when they surface naturally.

**1. Drive admins to invite more team members.** More invites push teams into the out-of-seats gate, which triggers seat purchases. We're testing invite prompts across surfaces (payment success, extension, admin pages) to increase the top of this funnel.

**2. Route new signups from existing domains to paying teams.** When a new user signs up from a domain that already has a paid team, we nudge them to join that team instead of starting fresh, converting organic signups into seat upsells without requiring admin action.

### The Story

Credit expansion is compounding. Three experiments rolled to 100% this week are collectively worth ~$1.25-1.3M in annualized ARR, and an early read on the Credit Progress Bar nav experiment shows Treatment A lifting FTP conversion +4% and credit purchases +34%. Seat expansion is steadier but bumpier: we hit the highest-ever seats purchased last week, yet a rollback on the invite suggestions experiment (which caused a 25% drop in seat purchases from the Users page) is a reminder that this surface is high-leverage and high-risk. The headline metric keeps climbing, with Expansion ARR at $2.18M, up 1.9% WoW and 9% MoM.

### What Changed and Why

Expansion ARR hit $2.18M last week, up 1.9% WoW and 9% MoM. Credits are the primary driver at $1.26M (+5% WoW), seats are steady at $720K (+1.6% WoW), and tier dipped to $200K (-13.4% WoW).

**Credit experiments rolling to full traffic.** Three experiments that were running at 50% rolled out to 100% this week, locking in meaningful ARR gains:

- Credits limit warning on Extension: +5% credit upsells, ~$150-200K annualized ARR
- Direct credit purchases from Manage Plan page: +5% credit upsells, ~$600K annualized ARR
- Credit usage bar on Extension: +11% credit upsells for paying users, +12% Record actioned events, ~$500K annualized ARR

**Credit Progress Bar nav experiment showing early signal.** We're testing 2 designs in the nav bar. Treatment A is showing +4% FTP conversion and +34% credit purchases. Treatment B is flat. Still early, but Treatment A looks promising.

**Seat expansion: mixed week.** We rolled back the Add invite suggestions to Users page experiment after it caused a 25% drop in seats purchased from the Users page, a surface that drives 50% of all seat upsells. That's a meaningful setback. On the positive side, the Add teammate on Payment success experiment is delivering +9% invites and +3% seat upsells and is rolling out. In-line invites and seat purchase on Extension is showing +12% invites and is still running.

### Supporting KRs at a Glance

**Seats added per week (target 789)** – **On Track** – Highest-ever seats purchased last week. Trend is moving in the right direction despite the Users page experiment rollback.

**Avg paid seats per team in first 4 weeks (target 1.22, from 1.11)** – **Off Track** – This metric has been flat for a year.

- **Problem:** Currently stuck around 1.11, no movement despite experiment wins in January.
- **Diagnosis:** Most seat expansion initiatives target the full user base, not specifically new teams in their first 4 weeks. The experiment wins are real but don't show up in this cohort-specific metric.
- **Plan:** Upcoming initiatives (in-app nudge to approve team joins, routing new users to existing teams) are designed to hit earlier in the team lifecycle and should move this cohort. Ships the week of 3/11.

### What's Next

**Seat expansion:** In-App Nudge to approve team joins (ship 3/11), Route new users to existing teams (ship 3/11), and Inline seat buying experience (ship 3/13). These three together target both pillars of the seat strategy: more invites and domain-based team joins.

**Credit expansion:** Credit Progress Bar nav experiment continuing to collect data. Treatment A results will inform whether we roll out or iterate.
