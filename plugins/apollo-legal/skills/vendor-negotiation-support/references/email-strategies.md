# Email Strategies

Use one of these when drafting vendor negotiation email responses.

## Hardline Close

When to use: Apollo has credible alternatives, the vendor is pushing an artificial deadline,
or the concession is small relative to vendor scale.

Template skeleton:

```text
Hi <name>,

Thanks for the updated proposal. We can move forward if the order form reflects:

1. <primary commercial ask>
2. <usage/credit/overage ask>
3. <deadline or renewal guardrail>

If those terms work, send revised paper and we can route it for approval. If they do not, we
should pause rather than rush a commitment that does not match current usage.

Best,
<sender>
```

Risk to avoid: Do not bluff a walk-away if the vendor is critical or Apollo lacks a credible
fallback.

## Conditional Fast Close

When to use: Apollo wants the vendor, timing matters, and the concession can be framed as the
condition for quick internal approval. Use this when Apollo is aligned on the structure, such
as UBB or a multi-year term, and only needs a sharper annual commit.

Template skeleton:

```text
Hi <name>,

This is close. If you can revise the proposal to include <specific concession>, I can help
route it quickly on our side.

The approval case is strongest with <annual commit / credit / term guardrail> because it keeps
the commitment aligned to measured usage while giving both teams room to expand.

Can you send an updated order form with that included?

Best,
<sender>
```

Risk to avoid: Do not promise signature or approval; promise only that the revised offer is
easier to route unless the user explicitly says they control the approval path and wants a more
committed close.

More committed close, when the user controls the path:

```text
If you can get this to <counter annual commit>/year flat for the <term>, with <credit/overage guardrails>, we can get this signed <timing>.
```

## Relationship-Preserving Counter

When to use: The vendor is strategic, switching cost is high, or Apollo needs support quality
after the negotiation.

Template skeleton:

```text
Hi <name>,

We value the partnership and want a structure that lets us keep expanding responsibly. The
current proposal is higher than we can justify against current usage, so we need to adjust:

1. <lower commit or phased ramp>
2. <rollover, opt-out, or overage guardrail>
3. <review date or expansion trigger>

That gives us a fair starting point now and a clear path to grow spend as usage proves out.

Best,
<sender>
```

Risk to avoid: Do not soften the actual ask so much that the vendor hears it as optional.

## Explanation Under The Draft

After the email, add a short reasonableness check:

```text
Why this is reasonable: <Vendor>'s proposal moves Apollo from <current ARR> to <proposed ARR> (<proposal net ARR> net ARR) and locks <proposal TCV> over <term>. The counter at <counter ARR> still gives them <counter net ARR> net ARR and the same structure, while asking for only <concession>/yr less. Apollo data suggests <vendor scale>, confidence: <confidence>.
```

If the negotiation is about UBB, state whether Apollo is accepting UBB:

```text
We are not pushing back on UBB; we are only trimming the annual commit.
```
