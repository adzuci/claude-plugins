# Competitive Positioning Reference

Use this only when a competitor is named, implied, or present in SFDC, Slack, Gong, or discovery notes. The Competitive Landscape section must focus on what matters for this specific deal. Generic positioning can supplement but cannot replace deal-specific evidence.

## Positioning Rules

- Start with the prospect's stated comparison criteria, not Apollo's generic strengths.
- Separate deal-specific evidence from standard battlecard guidance.
- Do not overclaim. If a competitor strength or weakness is not known for the deal, say `Unknown`.
- If a competitor is listed in SFDC but not discussed elsewhere, flag a competitive intel gap.

## ZoomInfo

Apollo generally wins on:

- All-in-one data plus engagement platform.
- Cost and consolidation value.
- Waterfall enrichment breadth and activation into workflows.

Watch-outs:

- ZoomInfo may be perceived as a data incumbent or enterprise-standard database.
- If the buyer frames evaluation purely as data accuracy, push for a Data Duel or benchmark with prospect data.

Recommended demo emphasis:

- Data and TAM enrichment.
- Waterfall enrichment.
- Browser extension and workflow activation.
- Cost-of-tool-sprawl consolidation if validated by deal context.

## Outreach and Salesloft

Apollo generally wins on:

- Data plus engagement consolidation.
- AI messaging and rep workflow in one platform.
- Cost and reduced vendor sprawl.

Apollo may be weaker on:

- Deep sequencing customization.
- Mature ecosystem integrations.
- Existing user muscle memory and migration comfort.

Watch-outs:

- No native migration path for sequences; manual migration effort may be required.
- Do not promise a frictionless migration unless validated.

Recommended demo emphasis:

- Sequencing and deliverability.
- AI messaging.
- Workflows and task management.
- Data-driven prospecting feeding directly into engagement.

## Cognism

Apollo generally wins on:

- Multi-region coverage.
- Engagement features beyond pure data.
- Price and platform consolidation.

Cognism may be stronger on:

- EU GDPR compliance positioning.
- Buyer perception around European data compliance.

Watch-outs:

- If EU data residency or GDPR is central, validate requirements with Security/Legal before making claims.

Recommended demo emphasis:

- Data and TAM enrichment.
- Waterfall enrichment.
- Signals and intent.
- Engagement workflows attached to data.

## Clay

Apollo generally wins on:

- Native engagement.
- CRM integration and sales execution workflows.
- Less operational complexity for standard GTM teams.

Clay is different because:

- Clay is a data orchestration layer, not a full engagement platform.
- Clay may be strong for technical RevOps teams that want custom enrichment workflows.

Watch-outs:

- Do not frame Clay as identical to Apollo. Clarify whether the buyer wants orchestration flexibility or sales-team execution.

Recommended demo emphasis:

- Native engagement from enriched data.
- CRM sync and workflow execution.
- Rep-ready usability without requiring RevOps to build every motion.

## Competitive Intel Gaps to Flag

Flag any of the following:

- Competition field populated but no decision criteria tied to competitor.
- Prospect mentioned a competitor but no strengths/weaknesses are known.
- AE claims a competitor is present but there is no source detail.
- Buyer is replacing Outreach/Salesloft and migration effort is not scoped.
- Buyer is comparing data providers but no Data Duel or benchmark is planned.

## Competitors Not Covered Here

If the deal names a competitor not covered in this reference, such as another data provider, engagement platform, intent vendor, CRM/platform vendor, conversation intelligence tool, or niche GTM tool, do not invent a battlecard. Use only deal-specific evidence from SFDC, Slack, Gong, or artifacts, and add: `Competitive positioning for [competitor] not in reference map — verify with PMM before making claims.`
