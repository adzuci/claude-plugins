# Do’s and Don’t for Marketing Content

With the exception of a few topics, marketing is generally free to draft content on any topic. However, there are a number of topics where marketing would need to collaborate closely with Legal in developing such content. See below for some do’s and don’ts related to particular topics that are at a high risk for litigation, **and please seek legal review of any content related to these topics.**

**Off Limit Topics**

- Apollo data scraping and data collection practices
- Compliance with laws and regulations (unless authored in collaboration with Legal)

**General Marketing Content Best Practices**

**DO’s:**

- DO engage in puffery (“Apollo is the most loved business data platform in the world”)
- DO showcase how Apollo can help customers grow their business (e.g. use words like “help” “facilitate” “makes it easier to”)
- Use customer experiences to showcase value by citing a testimonial for a specific customer with their consent or by using a generalized statistic substantiated and validated by Legal (e.g. on average, customers using Apollo see…)

**DON’Ts**:

- DON’T make guarantees about our product (e.g. “Apollo will speed up your sales motion” or “customers
- DON’T make unsubstantiated statements that can be verified and taken issue with (“Apollo has the most accurate data”)

**Do’s and Don’t for High Risk Topics**

| Topic | Do | Don’t |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| AI | Do highlight that AI can be tailored to customers based on input and context | Don’t suggest that models are “continually learning”, or being “trained” or “fine-tuned” (these are technical terms that need to be used accurately) |
| Cold Calling | Do emphasize compliance with laws, such as Do Not Call, (with Legal input) Do emphasize our products are for business and professional use | Don’t encourage spam-type calling Don’t suggest products can be used to reach “personal” or “private” numbers |
| Compliance | Do explain the laws accurately (with Legal input) | Don’t guarantee “compliance” |
| Competitors[^1] | Do state factual comparisons based on public data | Don’t use non-verified, non-public data about competitors Don’t make assumptions or statements based on conjecture about competitors |
| | Do stay professional, and use customer testimonials to highlight shortcomings of competitor solutions | Don’t smear competitors |
| | Do compare product features based on fact (with Legal input) | Don’t suggest that Apollo replicated a competitor’s specific product or that Apollo’s product is in “parity” with a competitor product |

# Product Demos and Screenshots

1. **I am creating a demo of the Apollo product, can I include real individuals’ data?**

   1. No. For any demos of the Apollo product, including organic social content, best practice is to use fake data. If there is personal data in a demo, it should be blurred out – including name, email, phone number, address and any other information that combined may identify that person.

1. **I am creating a demo of the Apollo product, can I include real company logos and data?**

   1. No. Companies often have protected trademarks for their name and logos, and if we use those trademarks in our marketing without their consent they could send us a cease and desist and ask us to take down the marketing content. Best practice is to use fake company names and logos.

1. **I am creating a demo of the Apollo product, can I use characters or celebrity names?**

   1. No. Celebrities have a right of publicity (i.e. the right to make money off the use of their name to commercialize their product), so we should not imply endorsement or use their name in marketing without their consent. Similarly, characters (e.g. Rachel Green, Bugs Bunny, etc.) are protected by intellectual property, and we cannot use those without consent of the IP owners.

# Emails

1. **Do we need to add unsubscribe links to marketing emails?**

   1. Yes - for all marketing emails or any email that contains promotional content. Transactional emails do not need unsubscribe links but they must be solely transactional in nature. It’s best practice to keep transactional emails as clean as possible and separate from marketing emails. If the marketing team has a message that they want to promote, they should send a separate email to users that are not unsubscribed.

1. **What are some examples of marketing and transactional emails?**

   1. Marketing Emails (subject to unsubscribe requirements):
      1. Promotional content about products/services
      1. Sales announcements or offers (e.g. trials, promotions, etc.)
      1. Upselling communications
      1. Newsletter content
      1. Event invitations for promotional purposes
   1. Transactional Emails (exempt from marketing unsubscribe):
      1. Order confirmations - "Your order #12345 has been confirmed"
      1. Account alerts - "Your password has been changed"
      1. Billing statements - "Your invoice is ready"
      1. Service updates - "Scheduled maintenance tonight"
      1. Security notifications - "New login detected"
      1. Subscription confirmations - "Welcome to our service"
      1. Password resets - "Reset your password"
      1. Legal notices - "Terms of service updated"

1. **If a customer has unsubscribed from email marketing, can we still send them transactional emails?**

   1. Yes. Transactional emails are not subject to CAN-SPAM unsubscribe requests. However, transactional emails still must be clearly transactional in nature, they cannot include marketing content or promotional material.

1. **Do we need opt-in consent for marketing emails?**

   1. No, when emailing individuals in the U.S., prior or opt-in consent is not required. However you must include an unsubscribe link and promptly honor unsubscribe requests (within 10 business days).

# Cold Calls

1. **What restrictions apply to cold calling in the U.S.?**

   1. While prior consent is generally not needed for marketing calls (so long as you are not using an automated dialing system), you must:
      1. Identify yourself as an employee at Apollo.
      1. Respect do-not-call requests
      1. Maintain internal do-not-call list
      1. Restrict calls to 8am-9pm local time

1. **Do we need to check Do Not Call lists before cold calling?**

   1. Yes. Apollo requires that you honor Do Not Call lists. Apollo’s Dialer has Do Not Call screening for the U.S..

1. **If a customer has unsubscribed from email marketing, can we still call them?**

   1. Yes. Email unsubscribe requests are channel-specific under US law. However, you must honor any specific requests not to call.

1. **What if a prospect tells me they no longer want to be called, but they aren’t on a DNC list?**

   1. You must still honor their request and place them on the internal do not call list.

# Buying Lists

1. **Can we buy lists of U.S. individuals?**

   1. Yes, but you assume compliance risk, as these lists likely are not clean. Best practice is to conduct diligence on the vendor as to their data collection methods. Additionally, you must scrub purchased lists against DNC lists before using and honor unsubscribe requests.

1. **What if the lists are from individuals of many different countries?**

   1. Please scrub the lists of individuals outside of the U.S.

1. **What due diligence should we perform when buying lists in the US?**

   1. You should:
      1. Verify data collection methods and consent
      1. Check list recency and accuracy
      1. Ensure vendor has proper data security protocols
      1. Get indemnification for compliance violations
      1. Test small samples before full purchase

# Giveaways, Contests and Sweepstakes

1. **What is a sweepstakes?**

   1. A sweepstakes (i.e. a giveaway) is a promotion that requires no payment to enter. Winners are chosen at random.

1. **What is a contest?**

   1. A contest is a promotion where the winner is determined based on skill. In a contest, there may be a clear winner based on the rules of the contest, or judges that determine a winner.

1. **Why can’t we call our promotion a raffle?**

   1. A raffle is considered a form of gambling under state law. Only non-profits and other certain types of businesses are allowed to host raffles. They often involve registration at the state level. It is preferable to call your promotion a “giveaway” or “sweepstakes”.

1. **Do I need any terms to host a giveaway or contest?**

   1. Yes. All sweepstakes/giveaways require Terms and Conditions, and an Affidavit signed by the winner(s). Please see this [Notion page](https://www.notion.so/apolloio/Sweepstakes-Giveaway-Templates-Instructions-b991a031d45245cdb9a7bd77d0146154?source=copy_link) for a self-serve template and instructions on how to complete it.

1. **What if I have questions on the Terms or Affidavit?**

   1. Reach out to Legal via the Legal Service Desk.

1. **Where should the Terms be linked?**

   1. They should be linked in the communication regarding the giveaway (i.e. on a webpage, in a social post, email, etc.)
