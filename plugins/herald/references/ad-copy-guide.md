# Ad Copy Frameworks

Quick-reference for writing ad copy that converts. Read alongside `apollo-voice.md`. These are not academic frameworks — they're practical tools that shape every headline, description, and CTA you write.

## 1. Awareness Levels (Schwartz)

The most important decision in ad copy: what does the prospect already know? Match your copy to their awareness level. Getting this wrong — leading with product details to someone who doesn't know they have a problem — wastes the ad.

| Level | They know... | Lead with... | Apollo example |
|-------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Most Aware** | Apollo + this feature. Need a reason to act now. | Offer, urgency, new proof point. Name the product directly. | "Apollo's AI sequences just got 3x faster. Upgrade now." |
| **Product Aware** | Apollo exists. Don't know this feature. | The specific capability and its outcome. Connect to what they already use. | "You're already prospecting in Apollo. Now your sequences write themselves." |
| **Solution Aware** | Tools like this exist. Don't know Apollo. | Differentiation. Why Apollo > alternatives? Proof and specifics. | "230M+ people. 97% email accuracy. One platform, no data vendor needed." |
| **Problem Aware** | They feel the pain. Haven't looked for solutions. | The pain. Agitate it. Then reveal the solution. | "Still copying leads from LinkedIn into spreadsheets? There's a faster way." |
| **Unaware** | Don't know they have a problem. | A startling fact, story, or identity statement. Longest path to conversion. | "The average SDR spends 65% of their day not selling." |

### Mapping awareness to channels

| Channel | Typical awareness | Why |
|---------|------------------|-----|
| Google Search (branded) | Most Aware | They searched for Apollo by name |
| Google Search (non-branded) | Solution Aware | They're searching for a category ("sales intelligence tool") |
| Bing Search | Solution Aware → Most Aware | Same intent profile as Google Search |
| Google Performance Max | Mixed (Problem → Solution Aware) | Google optimizes across surfaces; audience signals determine level |
| Google Demand Gen / YouTube | Problem → Unaware | Visual discovery surfaces (Discover, Gmail, YouTube). Earn attention with pain or a hook. |
| Meta | Problem Aware → Unaware | Feed-based; primary objective is registrations. Brand-forward video with product overlays + social proof has been the consistent winner. |
| Reddit | Problem → Solution Aware | Browsing communities related to their pain; some actively comparing tools. "Faux social post" style with snarky/self-aware tone is the standout format. |
| Performance TV (MNTN, TV Scientific) | Unaware → Problem Aware | Passive viewing; must earn attention with the pain or a startling claim |
| Affiliate (Partnerstack) | Mixed | Depends on the active offer and partner placement |
| Retargeting (any channel) | Most Aware → Product Aware | They've already visited; remind them why, handle objections |

**Rule:** Identify the awareness level for each audience-channel combination before writing copy. A/B variants should test within the same awareness level (e.g., two different pain angles for Problem Aware), not across levels.

### Cross-channel transferability

**Top and bottom performers do NOT reliably transfer across channels.** A creative or angle that wins on Reddit may flop on Meta, PMax, or Performance TV. When porting a winning creative from one channel to another, treat it as a fresh test — do not assume the learning carries over. Source: `references/paid-acquisition-playbook.md`.

### CTA standards by motion

CTAs are standardized by go-to-market motion, not by segment. See `references/paid-acquisition-playbook.md` for the full standard.

| Motion | Standard CTAs |
|--------|---------------|
| **SLG (sales-led)** | "Talk to Sales", "Request a Demo" |
| **PLG / self-serve** | "Get Started", "Sign Up Free", "Sign Up Now" |

## 2. Persuasion Principles (Cialdini)

Every ad should consciously deploy at least one. Name it. If you can't name the principle at work, the ad probably relies on nothing.

| Principle | What it does | When to use it | Apollo example |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Social Proof** | "Others like me trust this" | Almost always. Most reliable for B2B. | "Join 2M+ users on Apollo" / G2 badge / customer logos |
| **Authority** | "This source is credible" | Solution Aware audiences evaluating options | "230M+ verified contacts" / "#1 on G2 for Sales Intelligence" |
| **Reciprocity** | "They gave me value first" | Free plan CTAs, free tools, free credits | "Start free — 75 credits/month, real sequences, no credit card" |
| **Scarcity** | "I might miss out" | Retargeting, upgrade nudges, time-limited offers | "Your free credits reset in 3 days. Upgrade to keep building." |
| **Commitment** | "I already started, might as well continue" | Sequential funnels, landing page flow | "See how it works → Try it free → Build your first list" |
| **Unity** | "We're the same kind of person" | Identity-based targeting, community channels | "Built for founders who sell. Not enterprise sales orgs." |

**Rule:** Annotate each ad variant with the persuasion lever it deploys. "Variant A uses Social Proof (G2 rating in headline). Variant B uses Reciprocity (free plan CTA)."

## 3. Direct Response Principles

These are the copywriting fundamentals. Use them as a drafting guide and a review checklist.

### When drafting:

1. **One offer per ad.** Don't split attention. One CTA, one destination, one action. If you're torn between "Sign up free" and "Book a demo," make two variants — don't combine them.

1. **Specificity = credibility.** Every ad should include at least one concrete claim: a number, a timeframe, a named capability. "Find verified emails for 230M+ people" > "Access the world's largest database." Vague claims earn no trust.

1. **Strongest claim in the headline.** The headline is 80% of the ad's value (Ogilvy). Don't bury your best line in a description. If the prospect reads nothing else, the headline should be worth their attention.

1. **Enter the conversation already happening.** The ad should feel like a response to something the prospect was already thinking. For Google Search, this is literal — match the search query. For Reddit, match the community's language. For CTV, match a frustration they just experienced.

1. **PAS for pain-led copy.** Problem → Agitate → Solution. Don't just state the problem — twist the knife before offering relief. "Manual prospecting wastes hours. Every hour you spend copying contacts is an hour you're not closing. Apollo finds verified leads in seconds."

1. **Strong verbs, no adjectives.** "Find," "build," "close," "automate," "connect." Not "easy," "powerful," "seamless," "cutting-edge." The verb is the promise. The adjective is filler.

1. **CTA previews the outcome.** "Start finding leads — free" > "Sign up" > "Learn more." Tell them what happens when they click.

### When reviewing (self-check after drafting):

- [ ] Does every headline lead with pain, benefit, or proof — not a feature name?
- [ ] Does every ad include at least one specific, concrete claim (number, metric, timeframe)?
- [ ] Is there exactly one CTA per ad? Does it preview what happens next?
- [ ] Can you name the persuasion principle each variant deploys?
- [ ] Does the copy match the awareness level of the target audience on this channel?
- [ ] Would this ad make sense if the prospect had never heard of Apollo? (If it shouldn't, that's fine — but be intentional about it.)
- [ ] Is the strongest line in the headline, not buried in the description?
- [ ] Read it aloud: does it sound like a person talking, or a brochure?
