# Apollo Channel-Specific Guidelines

Detailed tone guidelines and examples for each marketing channel.

---

## Email
Tone: Direct, warm, and scannable.

### Subject line rules:
- Specific and benefit-driven. Name the thing. Not cryptic, not clever, clear.
- Keep between 45-55 characters.
- Incorporate a number, stat, or percentage where possible. We've seen ~20% more engagement when numbers are included.

### Preview text rules:
- Extends the subject, doesn't repeat it.
- Keep under 60 characters.

### Structure rules:
- H1: one punchy line. A direct statement or pointed question.
- H2: explains the "so what" in 1-2 sentences. Plain language only.
- Feature/section headlines: short. Noun + verb preferred.
- Body copy per section: 2-4 sentences max. One outcome per section. No jargon.
- Avoid paragraphs over 200 characters. Break into multiple paragraphs or bullets.
- Make email copy easily skimmable. Use headlines and sections.
- Mix short + medium sentences for rhythm.

### CTA rules:
- Direct action verb. "Reserve your seat" > "Learn More" > "Click here"
- Button text should clearly match what the user clicks off to. ~30 characters or less.
- End with one clean CTA and a clear reason to click.

### Signature rules:
- For newsletters and product updates: use "The Apollo Team" signature.
- For curated/personalized sends (Sales Community, Partners): use a specific Apollo employee the audience knows. Include a {Sender name} placeholder.

### Good subject + preheader examples:
- Subject: "4 Apollo updates to turn buying signals into action" / Preview: "Prioritize your best opportunities and make every outreach count."
- Subject: "3 can't-miss Apollo product updates for February" / Preview: "Just shipped: These updates help you capture more leads, stay organized, and move faster."
- Subject: "The AI research habit behind 47% more meetings" / Preview: "Plus: how one SDR sends 5x more emails in 75% less time."

### Good newsletter snippet examples:
- Headline: "5x more emails. 75% less time. Same personal touch." / Body: "Before Apollo, this Idomoo SDR spent an hour on every sequence. Now she builds them in 10 minutes, sends 5x more emails, and still holds a 6% response rate." / CTA: "See how they did it"
- Headline: "The 7 elements of a perfect cold email" / Body: "Sam McKenna built an 8-figure business on cold emails. In this guide, she breaks down the 7 elements behind emails that actually get replies, and why quality beats volume." / CTA: "Learn more"
- Headline: "How to close bigger deals with multiple stakeholders" / Body: "Looking to close bigger deals? This guide breaks down high-ticket sales and how to navigate longer sales cycles with multiple decision-makers." / CTA: "Read now"

### Bad vs Good patterns:
- **Bad email pattern**: too-long subject, sections with 4+ sentences each, multiple generic "Learn More" CTAs, ending with a vague webinar plug.
- **Good email pattern**: specific subject with a number, H1 as a pointed question, short 1-2 sentence sections, single strong CTA at the end ("Reserve your seat").

### Content newsletter body copy example edits:
- Original: "AI can handle the prospecting work most reps are still doing manually. In this how-to course, learn how to build an automated prospecting machine inside Apollo. You'll walk away with an AI-assisted SDR that handles research, personalization, and outreach on its own." / Edit: "AI can handle the prospecting work most reps are still doing manually. In this course, you'll learn how to build a prospecting machine in Apollo that researches accounts, personalizes outreach, and books meetings automatically."
- Original: "Sending more emails isn't the answer. The teams winning at outbound know which buyers are ready to act, what to say to them, and how to make sure that message lands in the inbox. Get the playbook below and see it in action." / Edit: "The teams winning in 2026 aren't blasting more emails. They know who's in-market, what messaging converts, and how to reach buyers at the right moment."

## Web / Product Copy
Tone: Confident, clear, zero fat.

### Core principles:
- Headlines state the outcome. Not what the feature is, what it does for you.
- Subheads explain the how. Plain terms, conversational, no jargon.
- Verb-led CTAs. "Start for free," "See it in action," "Build your list."
- Conversational. More "Never let a hot lead go cold again", less "Prioritize high-propensity accounts before they churn."
- No sentence should be doing nothing. Cut everything that doesn't move the reader forward.
- Subheads that don't add value: nix them. Less is more.

### Web copy rewrite examples:
- BAD H1: "Run Outbound End-to-End in Apollo With AI" / GOOD: "Run your entire outbound motion with AI"
- BAD: "Go from generic suggestions to expert-level GTM execution" / GOOD: "You describe the outcome. The AI Assistant executes."
- BAD: "Run the entire outbound motion, start to finish." / GOOD: "All things outbound, in one conversation"
- BAD: "It doesn't just tell you what to do. It actually does it for you in Apollo." / GOOD: "AI that doesn't just suggest, it actually executes"
- BAD: "Recommendations that fit your business, not generic templates" / GOOD: "Outputs that sound like you, not AI"
- BAD: "Apollo MCP brings your GTM stack inside the AI tools you're already using" / GOOD: "Turn your AI chat into an outbound workspace"
- BAD: "Build pipeline through conversations." / GOOD: "Turn your AI chat into an outbound engine"
- BAD: "One extension to rule them all" / GOOD: "Prospect anywhere you work online"
- BAD: "Apollo data to power your stack" / GOOD: "Power your stack with Apollo data"

## Magazine / Blog / Long-form
Tone: Narrative, human, editorial.

### Core principles:
- Shorter sentences for scannability.
- Less abstraction. Replace vague phrases with concrete outcomes.
- More rhythm. Mix short + medium sentences to keep it engaging.
- Selective line breaks. Avoid long paragraphs; use sparingly for emphasis.
- Lead with a specific moment, insight, or tension, not a generic industry observation.
- Avoid starting with "In today's world..." or "As [industry] continues to evolve..."
- Subheads should be punchy and tell a mini-story on their own.
- For case studies: keep them human-centered. Make the people the focus, not the product. Lead with success metrics. Include direct customer quotes. End with actionable takeaways.
- For selling-skills posts: provide actionable value, not just insight. Include templates and scripts when possible. Link to Apollo resources without overdoing it.
- Write like you're talking to a person, not describing a process. Don't just narrate what happens — make the reader feel it. Name the frustration. Acknowledge the experience. If a paragraph reads like a product manual, it needs a human moment.

## Social (LinkedIn, X/Twitter)
Tone: Sharp, credible, thought-leadery.

### Principles:
- First line must hook. If it doesn't land, nothing else matters.
- Use white space. One idea per line when possible.
- Don't write like a press release. Write like a smart person with something to say.
- Credibility through specificity: stats, real examples, counterintuitive takes.
- End with a point of view, not a soft CTA.
- Avoid: "Excited to announce," "Honored to share," "Thoughts?" with nothing after it.

## Reddit / Community Platforms
Tone: Witty, self-aware, a little sarcastic, still useful.

### Principles:
- Reddit can smell a marketing post. Write like a person, not a brand.
- Dry humor and sarcasm are welcome. Punch at the problem, never at the person.
- Still lead with something real and useful.
- Keep it short. Reddit wants a take, not a pitch.
- Self-aware: acknowledge the absurd or outdated behavior you're replacing.
- Dry delivery: no exclamation marks, no emojis, no hype.

### Good Reddit examples:
- "Still doing manual research for leads? That's so cool. Do you churn your own butter too?"
- "You scroll Reddit. Apollo scrolls the internet for leads."
- "Scrolling memes won't fill your pipeline. Apollo will."
- "Manually building pipeline in 2026. Bold strategy."
- "If you use Apollo and you're reading this, congrats on hitting your quota last month."
- "You spent 3 hrs researching 1 prospect? Our AI just researched 200 while you read this."

## Paid Ads (Display, Social Ads, SEM)
Tone: Urgent, punchy, zero tolerance for softness.

### Principles:
- One idea per ad. Full stop.
- Sentence case. No periods if it's one line.
- The hook must land in the first 3-5 words.
- No setup. Lead with the punchline or the payoff.
- State the outcome directly.
- Questions work if they're pointed and confident, not wishy-washy. BAD: "Is your outbound underperforming?" GOOD: "Your outbound could be better. Here's proof."
- Avoid soft CTAs. Prefer "Start now," "Get the list," "See it live."

### Bad vs Good ads:
- **Bad ads**: "Run outbound end-to-end in Apollo with AI" (too wordy), "More pipeline. Less busywork." (too vague)
- **Good ads**: "AI that doesn't just suggest. It executes." / "Close faster with AI-powered execution" / "Total pipeline visibility" / "All things outbound. One conversation."

## In-Product / UI Copy
Tone: Helpful, calm, human.

### Principles:
- Guide without condescending. Assume the user is smart.
- Error messages: empathy first, solution second. Never blame the user.
- Empty states: make them feel like opportunity, not failure.
- Labels: short nouns. Tooltips: one sentence.
