# Apollo 15-30-60 Day Onboarding Framework

## The 3-Phase Structure

Apollo's onboarding plan is organized around three milestone periods, each named after company values. These names are not just themes — they signal the mindset and behavior expected at each stage.

| Phase | Theme | Core Expectation |
|-------|-------|-----------------|
| Days 1–15 | Learn Voraciously & Be Customer Obsessed | Orientation, observation, and connection. The new hire's primary job is to listen, explore, and ask questions. |
| Days 16–30 | Take Ownership & Speak and Act Courageously | Testing ideas, contributing to small projects, building confidence. Goal is momentum, not mastery. |
| Days 31–60 | Move with Focus and Urgency & Be All for One | Acceleration and collaboration. Stepping fully into role responsibilities with more independence. |

______________________________________________________________________

## Day 1–15: Learn Voraciously & Be Customer Obsessed

**What this phase is about:** This first period is about orientation, observation, and connection. New hires are absorbing a lot — their primary job is to listen, explore, and ask questions.

**Focus Areas:**

- Understand company context and mission by completing the new hire orientation program in Sana
- Understand the team structure and how it collaborates cross-functionally
- Learn about products, services, or processes relevant to their role
- Start building relationships across the team and cross-functionally

**Prompts to guide objectives:**

- What internal documentation, tools, or resources should they review?
- Who should they shadow or meet with, and what context will that provide?
- What will help them understand how their role connects to the company's mission or strategy?

**Definitions of Success:**

- Curious and engaged in onboarding sessions
- Able to explain key aspects of the business or team structure
- Asks thoughtful questions and follows up with what they're learning

______________________________________________________________________

## Day 16–30: Take Ownership & Speak and Act Courageously

**What this phase is about:** The new hire begins to test ideas, contribute to projects, and build confidence. The goal is not mastery, but momentum.

**Focus Areas:**

- Contribute to a small workstream or scoped task
- Share early ideas or reflections from what they've observed
- Begin practicing the rituals, workflows, or processes of the team

**Prompts to guide objectives:**

- What's an achievable first contribution they can make?
- Where can they start applying what they've learned?
- What team practices or habits should they begin participating in?

**Definitions of Success:**

- Comfortable speaking up in meetings or 1:1s
- Beginning to own recurring tasks or responsibilities
- Offers feedback or suggestions respectfully

______________________________________________________________________

## Day 31–60: Move with Focus and Urgency & Be All for One

**What this phase is about:** Acceleration and collaboration. The new hire steps more fully into the role, operates with more independence, and actively partners with others.

**Focus Areas:**

- Take ownership of a scoped deliverable or area of responsibility
- Collaborate cross-functionally as needed for the role
- Reflect on strengths, opportunities, and feedback received so far

**Prompts to guide objectives:**

- What ownership opportunities can they take on?
- What does meaningful progress look like in their role by Day 60?
- Where might they begin contributing to team or cross-functional goals?

**Definitions of Success:**

- Operating independently on core tasks
- Taking initiative and following through
- Shows understanding of how their work connects to broader goals

## How to Write Good Objectives

For each phase, write 2–3 objectives. Each one should have:

1. **A clear, actionable goal** — written in checklist format so the new hire can check it off. Specific and concrete, not vague. Bad: "Learn about the product." Good: "Complete the Product 101 module in Sana and schedule a 30-min session with a product manager to ask questions about the current roadmap."

1. **Related competency** — which skill or behavior from the role's competency framework this objective builds toward

1. **Definition of success / progress indicators** — how you'll know this objective has been met

**Pacing principle:** Each phase should mirror the onboarding maturity curve. Don't front-load too much in Days 1–15 or leave too little for Days 31–60. Don't try to achieve all role outcomes within 60 days — focus on foundational progress toward long-term success.

## Milestone Check-ins

The day before each milestone (Day 15, 30, and 60), managers receive a Darwinbox notification reminding them to schedule a milestone check-in with their new hire. During check-ins, managers should:

- Reflect on how the new hire is doing based on the definitions of success
- Ask how they're feeling about their onboarding experience
- Discuss progress on the plan and make adjustments as needed

The plan is a starting point, not a fixed contract. Iteration is normal and expected.
