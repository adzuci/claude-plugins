Job search as market intelligence

Run a senior job search like research: written criteria, a repeatable sweep, and a report you can compare week over week.

AudienceStaff / Director / Principal candidates
Artifactcareer plugin
OutputA tier-ranked report in your vault

Senior roles exist, but the facts about level, pay and remote eligibility are scattered and often stale. Finding them is research, so I gave it a structure.

The career plugin has one skill, /career:market-sweep. It runs a job market intelligence sweep for a senior technical candidate and writes the result into your vault as a dated Markdown report.

How a sweep works

It starts from a criteria file (career/search-criteria.md in your vault): target titles, location and compensation floor, stack keywords, seniority signals, any priority filter, domain bonuses, and hard exclusions. The first run collects these; later runs confirm and reuse them, which is what makes the reports comparable over time.

The sweep itself covers market conditions for your level, priority companies with confirmed active roles, additional strong matches, stack trends in senior job descriptions, and compensation benchmarks. Claims are checked at the source: a "women-led" filter means checking who the CEO is, and a "Remote US" label means checking the company's careers page, not the aggregator.

The ranking answers one question: which five of these are worth an hour this week?

After writing the report, the skill reviews it as a recruiter would and revises it in place. It then offers to schedule a weekly repeat.

Limitations

It runs on web research. Compensation figures (Levels.fyi, Built In, Glassdoor) are rough, listings go stale, and you still have to judge each role yourself. The defaults assume remote US, $150k+ base and senior engineering or data titles; the criteria step lets you change them for other disciplines. Reports stay in your local vault, because a job search is private.

How to tell it is working: week three's report has tighter criteria and fewer, better-ranked companies than week one's, and you can see what changed.
